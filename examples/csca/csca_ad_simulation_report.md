# CSCA.ai Ad Simulation: Results

**What this is:** a MiroFish-style simulation that Claude ran directly, not the MiroFish engine. It used 42 AI personas: 14 each in Nepal, Bangladesh and Indonesia (9 students and 5 parents per country). Over 3 rounds they saw the 10 new ads, then commented and reacted to each other, then clicked, landed on the page and decided whether to sign up.

**How to read it:** this is a directional pre-test of messages and objections. It is not a forecast of real click or sign-up rates. The panel was picked to be relevant, so real rates will be much lower. Confirm the winners with a real Meta A/B test.

## Headline results

- **7 of 42 personas signed up.** All 7 were students; parents signed up 0 times.
- **4 of the 7 sign-ups came through retargeting (#7).** Most people need a second touch.
- **The biggest problem is the landing page, not the ads.** 6 of the 24 clicks bounced on slow mobile data, the Instagram in-app browser, or English-only text. One very motivated low-income student bounced twice.
- **Every unanswered comment was a lost lead.** Questions like "Is it a scam?", "Can management/commerce/IPS students apply?", "What does it cost?" and "Is the degree recognised?" led to drop-off whenever nobody replied.

## Top objections (all three countries)

1. **"Is this a scam, or will 'free' turn into a fee later?"** Students who were burned by fake scholarship pages or consultancy "processing fees" raise this most.
2. **"WiseAdmit is an agency, so this collects my number for sales calls."** The phone-number field on sign-up is a blocker.
3. **"Is this official (the Chinese government) or private?"** People confuse CSCA.ai, the official CSCA exam and the CSC scholarship.
4. **"Can management, commerce or IPS students apply?"** The ads assume science students.
5. **Parents: total cost, degree recognition (NMC/BMDC/UGC), safety for daughters, halal food, and a local office or WhatsApp contact.**
6. **English-only ads and landing page:** most parents and many district-town students are shut out.

## Real comments from the simulation

- 🇧🇩 "Free bole pore abar taka chaibe na to? Age o emon 'free' page dekhsi, pore 5000 tk processing fee 🙄"
  *"It says free, but they won't ask for money later, right? I've seen 'free' pages like this before, then a 5,000 taka processing fee."*
- 🇳🇵 "Phone number nadikana test dina milcha? Call aauna thalyo bhane delete garchu."
  *"Can I take the test without giving a phone number? If calls start coming, I'm deleting it."*
- 🇮🇩 "Min ini resmi dari pemerintah China atau bimbel? Ujung2nya bayar ya 😅"
  *"Admin, is this official from the Chinese government or a tutoring company? You end up paying in the end, right?"*
- 🇮🇩 Peer reply that rescued the thread: "Gw udah coba soal gratisnya, ga diminta bayar atau kartu kok."
  *"I already tried the free questions, it didn't ask for payment or a card."*
- 🇳🇵 Parent: "Is a Chinese degree recognised in Nepal (NMC/NEC)? The test is the last thing parents worry about."

## What to change (priority order)

1. **Fix the landing page first:** under 2 seconds on 3G, form above the fold, **Google or email sign-up with no phone number**, and a Bahasa version for Indonesia.
2. **Add a trust line to every ad:** "Free practice platform: no fee, no agent, no sales calls. Not affiliated with the official exam." Pin a comment with answers to the scam, fee and eligibility questions.
3. **Reply to every comment within a few hours** in romanised Nepali, romanised Bangla or Bahasa. This was the single biggest driver of trust in the simulation.
4. **Choose the creative per country:**
   - Nepal: #8 Reel, with a Nepali voiceover or captions
   - Bangladesh: #1 with Bangla copy, using static images rather than video
   - Indonesia: #10, the Bahasa headline
5. **Use #4 (sample question) for cheap reach, not sign-ups.** Lock the full solution and "see how you rank" behind sign-up, then retarget everyone who engaged with #7.
6. **Run #7 retargeting all the time,** but exclude parent audiences, and use a calmer version: "Your free test is still here. No payment, no calls."
7. **Change what the parent ads are for:** in local language, answer cost, recognition and safety, and use a "Send this to your child" CTA. Judge them on assisted sign-ups.
8. **Drop #9.** Consider a separate message for non-science students once you confirm which streams CSCA applies to.
