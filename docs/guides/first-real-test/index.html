Turn a simulated objection into a real experiment.

1. Inspect the objection and its source.

Open the saved dashboard’s Objections section. One generated buyer raises this concern:

The question to investigate is whether the billing explanation is clear. The quote alone does not establish how often real visitors have this concern or whether it causes them to abandon signup.

Check the product facts before writing a change. The fictional Fitloop offer in this run has a seven-day trial, requires a card, and then costs USD 4.99 per month. Keep those facts separate from a generated person’s interpretation.

2. Compare two truthful messages.

Try a price-first explanation while keeping the same offer. These examples contain the same facts in a different order:

Control
“7-day trial. Card required. Then USD 4.99/month.”
Variant
“USD 4.99/month after a 7-day trial. Card required.”

Illustrative wording for the fictional Fitloop offer. Replace it with verified terms for your own product, including any required billing and cancellation information.

The hypothesis is that putting the later charge first may make the offer easier to understand. Either version could perform better. Keep the price, audience and signup flow the same so the comparison focuses on the message order.

3. Decide what the real test will measure.

Assign real participants to one message at random. Ask the same comprehension question after each version: “What happens after the trial, and is a card required?” Define how an answer will be scored before reviewing responses.

Example primary measure: correct billing understanding

Participants who correctly identify the card requirement and post-trial price, divided by all participants assigned to that version who receive the question. Record incomplete answers rather than silently dropping them.

If you also observe trial starts, record their counts and eligible-visitor denominator separately. A clearer explanation and more signups are different outcomes; decide which one the experiment is meant to answer.

Choose a guardrail relevant to your product, such as billing-related support requests. Agree on the sample, end condition, analysis and decision rule before looking for a winner. Report uncertainty and any changes made during the test.

The saved simulation does not supply real experiment results. Bring actual outcomes back only after collecting them, and retain the distinction between simulated reactions and observed behavior.

4. Keep the observation and the outcome in one record.

Use this worksheet for the next real test. Replace the example with your own facts, then keep the original plan when you add results.
